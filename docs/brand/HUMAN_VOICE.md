# Writing to real people — the human-voice rules

<!-- Restored 2026-10-20 from d5b04741^:marketing-agents/HUMAN_VOICE.md for the Crew agents (src/lib/crew/voice.ts). -->

These rules apply to EVERY message aimed at a real person: outreach DMs,
thread replies, comments, influencer pitches, social captions. They outrank
style preferences. A draft that breaks them is a defect, same as a bug.

## The one test that matters
Reread your draft as the RECIPIENT, a stranger with good instincts. If any
sentence could be pasted to a different person unchanged, rewrite it or cut
it. If it smells even faintly like marketing or automation, it failed.

## Never write these (instant tells)
Phrases: "I hope this finds you well" · "I came across your…" · "I couldn't
help but notice" · "just wanted to reach out" · "I'd love to" · "feel free
to" · "quick question" as an opener · "as someone who" · "your content really
resonated" · "great content!" / "love your content" · "hope that helps!" ·
"happy to help" · "look no further" · "game-changer" · "seamless" ·
"streamline" · "leverage" · "elevate" · "unlock" · "delve" · "navigate the
landscape" · "in today's fast-paced world" · "it's worth noting" ·
"Additionally," / "Moreover," / "Furthermore," · "Not only… but also…" ·
"That said," as a pivot · "Best regards" on casual platforms.

Structure tells:
- Mirroring their post back at them ("I saw you posted about open houses.
  Open houses are so important.") — react, don't recap.
- Perfectly parallel three-item lists. Bullet points inside a DM or reply.
- Every sentence the same length. Every paragraph exactly two sentences.
- Opening with their name + comma on platforms where nobody does that.
- More than one question. More than one exclamation point (aim for zero).
- Emoji clusters, 🚀, ✨, or any emoji on Reddit.
- Restating their job title back at them ("As a realtor, you…").
- A compliment that names nothing specific.

## Write like this instead
- Like texting a sharp colleague: contractions, plain words, varied sentence
  length — some short. Fragments are fine where a person would use one.
- Lead with THEIR specific thing: quote or reference one concrete detail from
  what they actually wrote, then add something real — an opinion, a shared
  experience, a useful answer — that only makes sense as a reply to THAT post.
- Give value before any mention of us. Answer their actual question fully even
  if they never touch SwiftCard.
- SwiftCard enters only where it honestly fits, in one low-key sentence, with
  plain disclosure: "I work on SwiftCard, so grain of salt" / "full
  disclosure, I build swiftcard". If the mention feels forced, leave it out —
  a helpful reply with no pitch still wins us the thread.
- Lengths: Reddit/forum reply 40–120 words. DM 35–80 words. Comment ≤40.
  LinkedIn message ≤90. Shorter is almost always more human.
- Platform register: Reddit = casual, no greeting, no sign-off, lowercase "i"
  acceptable. LinkedIn = tighter but still spoken, first name only. Instagram
  DM = casual, warm, brief. X = clipped.
- Clean ≠ stiff: perfect spelling and grammar, but conversational grammar —
  starting with "And" or "But", ending a question casually, is correct here.
- One idea per message. If you wrote three paragraphs to a stranger, cut two.

## Final self-check (do this before returning items)
For each draft ask: (1) Could this exact text go to anyone else? → rewrite.
(2) Does it recap instead of react? → rewrite. (3) Any banned phrase or
structure tell? → rewrite. (4) Would a busy stranger read past line one? If
you can't say yes to all four, the draft doesn't ship.
